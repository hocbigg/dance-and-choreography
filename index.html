Path to a free self-taught education in Dance And Choreography.

Last updated on: 2026-10-04

Introduction

Dance and choreography unite physical discipline, somatic awareness, and compositional architecture. The discipline is concerned not merely with executing steps, but with understanding how the body articulates through space and time, how movement communicates meaning, and how to craft cohesive choreographic works from raw physical ideas. This curriculum provides a rigorous, self-directed foundation for absolute beginners. It assumes no prior studio training, formal dance background, or music literacy.

Because dance is an embodied art form, it cannot be learned through reading alone. The curriculum is structured around two complementary tracks that should be studied concurrently rather than strictly end-to-end:

Physical practice and safe alignment form the first track. You should begin your physical training with foundational barre work to establish postural stability, foot articulation, and turnout mechanics, while simultaneously working through dance anatomy. Studying anatomy and kinesiology alongside daily studio drills allows you to understand skeletal alignment and muscular engagement from the inside out, establishing safe movement habits and preventing injury from day one.

Analytical literacy and creative composition form the second track. Music fundamentals and dance history train your ear and eye. Music literacy teaches you to decipher meters, rhythmic subdivisions, and dynamic phrasing so you can collaborate effectively with musical scores. Dance appreciation builds the analytical vocabulary and historical context required to critically evaluate concert works. These skills converge in choreography and composition, where you translate movement vocabulary, musical sensitivity, and spatial design into original studies and finished pieces.

This curriculum covers strictly the foundational core that every dance practitioner needs before branching into specific traditions or advanced techniques. Once you have established these fundamentals, you can pursue specialized movement styles, somatic systems, and technology in Advanced Topics, apply your knowledge to concrete deliverables such as staging a piece or archiving work in Projects.

Curriculum

Dance Technique Foundations

The daily physical practice of dance technique, building somatic awareness, alignment, strength, and movement vocabulary through structured class work.

Dance skills (Open University/OpenLearn) - A free introductory module establishing the principles of safe dance practice, postural alignment, warm-up physiology, and performance focus to study before and alongside physical studio drills.

Beginner Ballet Barre and Classes (Kathryn Morgan/YouTube) - The primary follow-along video resource for absolute beginners, teaching core positions, spinal alignment, foot articulation, and basic barre exercises at a deliberate, accessible pace.

Online Ballet Classes (Dutch National Opera & Ballet/YouTube) - Builds directly on Kathryn Morgan's introductory material; use this professional company series to transition into full-length barre and center work once basic alignment and terminology are consistent.

Dance Anatomy and Safe Practice

How the dancer's body functions mechanically, focusing on joint architecture, muscular engagement, injury prevention, and conditioning for long-term physical sustainability.

Dance Anatomy (Jacqui Greene Haas/Human Kinetics) - The more visual, applied option between the two anatomy texts, combining illustrated muscle-activation diagrams with targeted conditioning exercises that connect directly to standard studio steps.

Dance Anatomy and Kinesiology (Karen Clippinger/Human Kinetics) - An alternative to Haas for learners wanting a deeper scientific foundation, offering detailed biomechanical analysis of skeletal deviations, joint mechanics, and injury pathology across dance forms.

Dance History and Appreciation

The foundational traditions, choreographers, and cultural contexts that shaped concert dance, paired with frameworks for viewing, describing, and critically analyzing choreographed work.

Dance Appreciation (DAN 1025) (OER Commons/Red Rocks Community College) - A comprehensive open survey introducing the core elements of dance—body, energy, space, time, and action—and examining how meaning is generated across concert, vernacular, and world dance traditions.

Traditions in American Concert Dance: Gender and Autobiography (MIT OpenCourseWare) - Sequential follow-up to the introductory survey above, providing syllabus readings and analytical prompts focused on the seminal 20th-century choreographers who defined modern concert dance.

Music Fundamentals for Dancers

The practical mechanics of music—meter, rhythm, tempo, dynamics, phrasing, and form—and how choreographers interpret, count, and collaborate with musical structures.

Music Fundamentals for Dance (Nola Nolen Holland/Human Kinetics) - A comprehensive text designed specifically for dancers, providing practical exercises in rhythmic notation, score analysis, metric structures, and musical phrasing tailored to choreographic planning.

Choreography and Composition

The craft of generating and structuring movement into finished dances, exploring time, space, energy, motif development, and formal compositional architecture.

The Intimate Act of Choreography (Lynne Anne Blom and L. Tarin Chaplin/University of Pittsburgh Press) - The primary foundational workbook for dance making, using sequential improvisations and structured assignments to guide the learner from basic movement exploration through compositional devices and complete works.

Dance Composition: A Practical Guide for Teachers (Jacqueline M. Smith-Autard/A & C Black) - Complements Blom and Chaplin by providing a clear, structural framework for motif selection, variation, phrasing, and stage spatial design, available to borrow freely via the Internet Archive.

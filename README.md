# Curriculum

This curriculum covers the foundational subjects that every learner in dance and choreography needs before specializing. It moves from physical practice through theoretical context to creative application, assuming no prior formal training.

## Dance Technique Foundations

The daily physical practice of dance technique, building body awareness, alignment, strength, and movement vocabulary through structured class work.

[Dance skills (Open University/OpenLearn)](https://www.open.edu/openlearn/education-development/dance-skills) - a free, self-paced short course covering safe dance practice, posture, alignment, combinations, and performance skills. It works well as the theoretical companion to your studio practice.

[Beginner Ballet Barre and Classes (Kathryn Morgan/YouTube)](https://www.youtube.com/@KathrynMorgan) - a professional ballerina's channel with follow-along barre and center work for complete beginners. Use the beginner-level barre videos as your daily technique practice; search "Kathryn Morgan beginner ballet" on YouTube to find the introductory classes.

[Online Ballet Classes (Dutch National Opera & Ballet/YouTube)](https://www.youtube.com/@DutchNationalOperaBallet) - free recorded classes taught by company members, ranging from beginner to more experienced levels. Once you are comfortable with Kathryn Morgan's beginner material, these offer a step up in difficulty and a taste of professional-level class structure.

## Dance Anatomy and Safe Practice

How the dancer's body works — skeletal alignment, muscular engagement, injury prevention, and conditioning practices that support long-term physical health.

[Dance Anatomy (Jacqui Greene Haas/Human Kinetics)](https://books.google.com/books?isbn=9780736081931) - an illustrated guide linking anatomy directly to dance movement, showing which muscles activate during specific steps and how to train them safely. Read it alongside your technique practice rather than front-to-back.

[Dance Anatomy and Kinesiology (Karen Clippinger/Human Kinetics)](https://books.google.com/books?isbn=9781492545170) - a more detailed kinesiology text covering joint structure, movement analysis, and common alignment problems. Either this or Haas works as your primary anatomy reference; Clippinger goes deeper into the science, while Haas is more visual and immediately practical.

## Dance History and Appreciation

The major traditions, choreographers, and works that shaped concert dance, developing the ability to watch, describe, and analyze dance intelligently.

[Traditions in American Concert Dance: Gender and Autobiography (MIT OpenCourseWare)](https://ocw.mit.edu/courses/21m-670-traditions-in-american-concert-dance-gender-and-autobiography-spring-2008/) - a complete undergraduate course with syllabus, readings, and assignments exploring the artists and traditions that shaped American concert dance, from Isadora Duncan and Martha Graham to Katherine Dunham and Alvin Ailey.

[Dance Appreciation (DAN 1025) (OER Commons/Red Rocks Community College)](https://oercommons.org/courseware/lesson/123196) - a full open educational resource course introducing the fundamental elements of dance (body, energy, space, time, action) across diverse genres. Use it as your structured survey of the field; the MIT course then narrows the focus to American concert dance specifically.

## Music Fundamentals for Dancers

How music works — rhythm, structure, phrasing, and dynamics — and how dancers and choreographers listen to and work with musical scores.

[Music Fundamentals for Dance (Nola Nolen Holland/Human Kinetics)](https://books.google.com/books?isbn=9780736096522) - the standard text for dancers on music literacy, covering form, structure, time signatures, melody, and score reading through exercises directly relevant to dance performance and choreography. It is the only book-length resource purpose-built for this subject, and no free equivalent of comparable quality exists.

## Choreography and Composition

The craft of creating dance — manipulating time, space, and energy; developing movement material; structuring phrases; and building complete works.

[The Intimate Act of Choreography (Lynne Anne Blom and L. Tarin Chaplin/University of Pittsburgh Press)](https://books.google.com/books?isbn=9780822934639) - a widely used guide that teaches choreography through structured improvisations and studies, starting from the basics of time, space, and force and progressing to compositional structures and choreographic devices.

[Dance Composition (Jacqueline Smith-Autard)](https://archive.org/details/dancecomposition0000smit) - a complementary approach to choreography that emphasizes the creative process and the dancer-as-maker, available to borrow for free from the Internet Archive. Use it alongside Blom and Chaplin for a broader view of compositional methods.

## Curation Summary

**Dance Technique Foundations** — core because no dancer can progress without physical training. Free/OpenLearn course plus free YouTube technique classes make this subject entirely free.

**Dance Anatomy and Safe Practice** — core because every dancer needs to understand their instrument and prevent injury. Both recommended books are paid; I checked for free anatomy courses for dancers and found only scattered videos rather than structured courses, so the books remain the best options.

**Dance History and Appreciation** — core because contextual literacy separates a dancer from a mover. Both resources are free (MIT OpenCourseWare and OER Commons), making this subject fully accessible.

**Music Fundamentals for Dancers** — core because choreography demands musical literacy. The one canonical textbook is paid; I searched for free music-for-dancers courses and open textbooks and found none that matched its scope and dance-specific focus.

**Choreography and Composition** — core because it is the discipline's creative practice. One book is paid, the other is freely available via the Internet Archive, giving a balanced free-paid mix.

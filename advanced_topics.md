# Advanced Topics: Dance and Choreography

This page branches out from the Core Curriculum into distinct specialization tracks for learners who have completed foundational training and want to deepen their practice in a specific direction.

## Track Index

- **Dance Science and Somatics** - The scientific study of the dancer's body and mind-body integration practices that enhance technique and prevent injury.
- **Contemporary and Postmodern Choreography** - The experimental choreographic methods and movement research that reshaped dance from the 1960s onward.
- **World and Vernacular Dance Traditions** - Deep study of specific cultural dance forms beyond Western concert dance, including their rhythms, techniques, and social contexts.
- **Dance for Camera and Technology** - Creating dance for the screen and using digital tools for choreographic visualization and notation.

## Dance Science and Somatics

This track goes beyond basic anatomy into evidence-based conditioning, injury prevention science, and somatic movement practices that refine proprioception and body awareness.

Injury Prevention Science: [The Efficacy of Physical Fitness Training on Dance Injury (Y. Dang et al./PubMed Central)](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9564914/) - an open-access systematic review examining how physical fitness training affects injury rates, pain intensity, and dance performance, providing the evidence base for designing conditioning programs.

Feldenkrais Method: [Feldenkrais Project Lesson Collections (Nick Strauss-Klein/Feldenkrais Project)](https://feldenkraisproject.com/collections) - over 50 free Awareness Through Movement audio lessons sourced from Moshe Feldenkrais's original 1972 book, offering structured self-guided study in somatic re-education for dancers.

Laban Movement Analysis: [Laban's Efforts in Action (Vimeo Channel)](https://vimeo.com/channels/1391433) - a collection of 47 videos offering practical exercises, case studies, and theoretical background for learning the Effort factor (weight, time, space, and flow) in movement observation and analysis.

## Contemporary and Postmodern Choreography

This track covers the experimental practices that fundamentally challenged how dance is made — from contact improvisation to chance operations to site-specific work.

Contact Improvisation: [Steve Paxton Talking Dance (Walker Art Center/YouTube)](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=_82Od5NM4LI) - a 100-minute performative dialogue with the creator of contact improvisation, including performance and discussion of his influential work, providing direct access to the form's origins and philosophy.

Postmodern Dance History: [Judson Dance Theater: The Work Is Never Done (Museum of Modern Art)](https://www.moma.org/calendar/exhibitions/5089) - the online component of MoMA's landmark 2018 exhibition documenting the 1962-1964 Judson collective that stripped dance of theatrical conventions, featuring videos, artist biographies, and performance documentation.

Chance Operations: [Merce Cunningham's Working Process (Walker Art Center/YouTube)](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=zhK3Ep4HiI0) - a short documentary in which Cunningham explains his use of chance procedures (dice, coins, charts) to determine movement sequence, spatial patterns, and dancer assignments, with reflections on how this freed choreography from personal habit.

Site-Specific Performance: [On Site: Methods for Site-Specific Performance Creation (Stephan Koplowitz/Oxford University Press)](https://books.google.com/books?isbn=978019751524X) - a practical methods book by an award-winning site choreographer covering how to research a site, develop movement specific to its architecture and history, and navigate the production challenges of non-theatrical spaces.

## World and Vernacular Dance Traditions

This track deepens engagement with specific cultural dance forms, emphasizing the inseparability of movement from its musical and social contexts.

West African Dance: [Online West African Dance Class with Maguette Camara (Ailey Extension/YouTube)](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=AeJlb-n89LA) - a 36-minute class breaking down fundamentals of traditional West African dance with emphasis on understanding the accompanying djembe drum rhythms, taught by a former member of the Ballet Bougarabou Dance Company in Senegal.

Hip-Hop Foundations: [How I Practice Hip Hop Dance with Four Foundations (Jeffery Hu/YouTube)](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=GcZJMiHds3U) - a widely viewed tutorial covering the four foundational elements of hip-hop dance (bounce, rock, groove, and freestyle) with clear demonstrations of how to practice each at home.

## Dance for Camera and Technology

This track explores the intersection of dance with digital media — both creating choreography for the screen and using software tools for choreographic visualization and preservation.

Screendance Theory: [Screendance: Inscribing the Ephemeral Image (Douglas Rosenberg/Oxford University Press)](https://books.google.com/books?isbn=9780199772612) - the foundational monograph defining screendance as a distinct hybrid form, covering its history, theory, and the unique properties that differentiate dance made for the screen from stage performance.

Choreographic Software: [DanceForms (Credo Interactive/CharacterMotion)](https://www.charactermotion.com/df-download.html) - a 3D choreographic visualization tool (developed with Merce Cunningham) now freely available for download, allowing you to sketch and animate dance sequences using poseable figures and movement libraries.

Dance Notation: [Labanotation Fundamentals (Dance Notation Bureau)](https://www.dancenotation.org/labanotation-fundamentals) - a free interactive introduction to the Labanotation system for recording human movement, teaching how symbols on a staff convey direction, body parts, levels, and timing.

## Curation Summary

**Dance Science and Somatics** - included because advanced dancers need both the scientific evidence for conditioning and the somatic practices that refine movement quality beyond technique class. All three resources are free and open-access, making this track fully accessible.

**Contemporary and Postmodern Choreography** - included because these methods fundamentally changed how dance is created and are now standard vocabulary in contemporary practice. Three of four resources are free (YouTube videos and MoMA's online exhibition); the site-specific book is paid but I checked for free alternatives and found none that matched its practical methods coverage.

**World and Vernacular Dance Traditions** - included because specialization requires engagement with forms beyond Western concert dance, and these traditions emphasize musical-literacy skills the core curriculum only introduces. Both resources are free YouTube classes, making this track entirely accessible.

**Dance for Camera and Technology** - included because screen-based dance and digital tools are now central to professional practice, yet are absent from core training. The balance is mixed: DanceForms software and Labanotation fundamentals are free; the Rosenberg screendance monograph is paid but is the field-defining text with no adequate free equivalent covering the same scope.
